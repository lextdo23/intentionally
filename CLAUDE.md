# Intentionally: notes for Claude

The owner, alex, has no coding experience. Explain changes in plain English, no jargon.

## What this app is (signed off by alex, 2026-11-03). Check every idea against this first.
Full one-pager: https://claude.ai/artifact/3YMBhpwDit9UNVWP2V2qHY

- **Purpose:** live with intention. Choose the few parts of life that matter, give them time, and see where your time really goes, a week at a time.
- **Built for:** people aged 18 to 30, burnt out or low (not in crisis), in Australia first: young workers early in their careers (Mia, 24) and university students (Josh, 19).
- **The rule:** a feature or fix goes in only if it helps them, or costs them nothing (no extra steps, nothing extra on screen). Others are welcome, but we don't design around them.
- **The core loop (time leads; everything else supports it):**
  1. Choose: 3 to 5 pillars, 2 of them focus, chosen on day one (the app helps people choose).
  2. Give time: tap a planet to time it, or log "I gave time" afterwards with a rough length. Both count the same.
  3. See: tap the sun any time for "Your week" (each planet's time this week). No numbers on the sky itself (alex, 2026-11-03). Lead with what happened, never gaps, comparisons or targets.
  4. Reflect: the weekly look back opens on the chosen day and waits until the person is ready.
- **Supporting features, one job each:** the sky (shows life at a glance), the weekly look back, catching thoughts (where your mind goes, beside where your time goes), sorting thoughts, words of affirmation (kind, believable lines tied to their pillars; for kind, worry and want thoughts; never for heavy thoughts), seasons, safety.
- **Not:** a productivity tracker, a journal first, or therapy.
- **Social (agreed with alex, 2026-11-20; being designed):** friends and small communities supporting each other to live with intention. Seen and supported, never measured. Profiles: picture, a name (needn't be real), @username, an optional "This season" line, pillars only if they choose, recent moments they shared, and a friends count (alex wants it; friends are mutual, never one-way followers). Moments: nothing is shared automatically; after a check-in, a card or time given, a quiet "Share with friends?"; a moment is a pillar given time, a card finished or a photo of the day, with optional words. One warm reaction ("I see you"), never counted likes. A calm list of friends' moments, no endless feed. Circles: small groups (about 12) around a shared intention; anyone can start one and invite friends. No rankings, leaderboards, streaks or "you're behind". Safety: private by default, block and report, checking of new or reported content, Lifeline one tap away, adults only, privacy policy updated before launch. Decided with alex (2026-11-20): accounts are private by default, with an option to go public; each moment is shared with friends (default), a circle, or everyone (public accounts only). Inspiration: public moments grouped by pillar (Rest, Move, Study...), never by popularity, with no counts; anyone can hide it. Friend requests can be sent to public and private accounts, with an option to turn requests off. Comments only between friends; strangers can only send a request or an "I see you". Circles are invite-only (never listed or searchable); people find each other through Inspiration, become friends, then invite each other in. Public moments are reported, checked and blockable (Apple requires this for shared content). No sun/orange accent on the social screens (alex 2026-11-20): main buttons off-white (#efede9) with dark text, everything else quiet greys; colour only in pillar dots, photos and profile pictures. The rest of the app keeps its sun buttons for now. Getting around (alex 2026-11-20): one floating see-through glass pill at the bottom: Today · Friends · You (icons) | the screen's main action (End your day on Today, Share a moment on Friends). Daydeck always opens on Today; the pill hides during check-ins and cards; Settings moves to You; Friends starts with "How did today go?"; no badges or counts, just a small dot. Look: use familiar social-app layouts (profile grid, round pictures, bottom bar) in Daydeck's own style; never Instagram's name, logo, icons or a near-copy of its screens (Apple guideline 4.1). Day photos get backed up after the social side is built; profile photos go to the server with it. Update the one-pager to match.
- **Review gates:** after planning, after the basic build, and after two weeks of real use, check that people can (1) say what it's for in one sentence, (2) know what to do tomorrow, (3) want to come back, (4) feel no homework or guilt. A failure fixes the one-pager first, never a patch on top.

## Tech
- The app is Daydeck, served at **https://daydeck.day** (alex, 2026-11-20): GitHub Pages with a custom domain (DNS on Cloudflare). Daydeck lives at the front of the repo; the old Intentionally app is retired and removed. `v2/` only forwards old links and icons on to the front page, keeping `?tester`.
- Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules. No build step, no frameworks, no dependencies. Open `index.html` through any static server.
- All data lives on the phone: `localStorage` under the key `daydeck` (see `js/store.js`), photos in IndexedDB (`js/photos.js`). Bump `SCHEMA` and migrate when the shape changes.
- **Photos keep nothing but the picture** (alex 2026-11-20): no location, time, phone or camera details, and no note of when a photo was taken or whether it came from the camera or the library. Every photo coming in or going out (camera, library, Restore, Save a copy, Save my photos, share, and later a profile photo) goes through `clean()` in `js/photos.js`. The app never asks for access to the photo library; it uses the phone's own picker, which hands over only the one photo chosen. `node tests/photo-clean.test.cjs` checks it.
- `sw.js` caches the app for offline use. **Bump `VERSION` and update `FILES` whenever any file changes or is added.**
- iOS 26 lays a home-screen app out one status bar short (a strip at the bottom) unless the page is taller than that; `html` gets a `min-height` for home-screen apps in `css/app.css`. Keep it.
- Every page (`index.html`, `testers.html` and the `v2/` forwarding page) carries a Content-Security-Policy meta tag (safety net): only the app's own files and `intentionally-push.lextdo.workers.dev`. No inline `<script>` and no outside scripts; a new outside service must be added to `connect-src` in `index.html` and `testers.html`. `node tests/csp.test.cjs` checks every screen.
- Time always comes from `now()` in `js/clock.js`, never `Date.now()` directly in app logic, so test mode's time travel works. (The sky's orbit phase uses the real clock on purpose.)

## Accounts (decided with alex, 2026-11-20; live for everyone)
- **Sign up first, with a way out.** New people see "Keep your days safe" before choosing pillars: email, then a 6-digit code, then a username, then Face ID. "Use without an account" is always there; it shows one calm screen saying what they give up (days live only on this phone, so a lost phone loses them; friends and sharing will need an account) and that they can sign up any time in Settings. People already using the app see the sign-up screen once after the update; their days upload when they sign up. This is a deliberate exception to "no extra steps".
- **Signing in:** passkeys (Face ID, Touch ID or the phone passcode), with no passwords. A 6-digit email code is for first sign-up and for getting back in if the passkey is missing. Always a code, never a link: on iPhone a link would open Safari, not the home-screen app. The code box uses `autocomplete="one-time-code"` so iPhone offers the code from Mail.
- **Usernames:** 3 to 20 characters, letters, numbers, full stops and underscores only (plain a to z, so no code, emoji, invisible characters or lookalike letters from other alphabets); not starting or ending with a full stop, no two in a row, at least one letter (alex 2026-11-20, like Instagram); capitals don't count ("Mia" = "mia"); numbers standing in for letters ("adm1n") are read as letters when checking blocked words; blocked: offensive words, anything posing as Daydeck or staff ("daydeck", "admin", "support", "official", "help"), and crisis-line names. Taken names get two or three suggestions. Can change once every 30 days; an old name stays locked for a while. Not shown to anyone until the social side exists. It doesn't have to be a real name, and the screen says so. A display name waits for the social side.
- **What the server keeps:** email, username, the passkey's public half, sessions, and each person's backed-up days. Normal accounts: protected, kept to the minimum, never read or sold; the privacy policy says so plainly. Photos stay on the phone for now (they still travel in Save a copy), including profile photos until the social side exists; backing them up is planned (see "Photos will be backed up too"). Typed words are part of the backup, but tester mode still never sends typed words.
- **Backup and sync** (`js/sync.js`, built 2026-11-20): the phone stays the main copy and works offline; changes go up a few seconds after they're made, whenever it's online, and come back on a new phone. Everything is split into pieces (each day, and each other part like pillars or levels), and the newer piece wins, so a day changed on one phone never wipes a different day changed on another. A new phone's blank start never overwrites the backup. "Backed up 5 minutes ago" in Settings. Stays on the phone only: tester mode, test mode, notifications, photo settings, the card library's caches. Sign out keeps the phone's data. Delete everything (or a phone suddenly emptied) signs that phone out and never wipes the backup. Delete account erases everything on the server. The server refuses to overwrite a backup a phone hasn't seen; it sends it back to merge. Backup sync state lives under `localStorage` key `daydeck-sync`. `node tests/sync.test.cjs` runs two pretend phones against the real service.
- **Photos will be backed up too (alex 2026-11-20, not built yet).** Plan: their own store (Cloudflare R2, one picture per day plus the profile photo), not inside the days backup (capped at about 1.5 MB). Every photo goes through `clean()` first and is sent shrunk; only its owner can read it (and later, friends they allow); deleted when changed or with the account. The day's note then says the photo is backed up, and a new phone fetches pictures as they're shown. Until then a day's `ph` note is kept out of the backup on purpose. Update the privacy policy and Settings words ("Photos stay on this phone") when this is built.
- **Privacy policy** (`privacy.html`, daydeck.day/privacy.html, 2026-11-20): plain English, written to cover every topic the Australian Privacy Principles (APP 1.4) ask for, since Daydeck may count as a health service. Linked from the first sign-up screen and Settings → About. **Keep it true:** any change to what leaves the phone, who helps run Daydeck, or how long things are kept must update it and its date (and say so in the app if it's a big change, like backing up photos).
- **Where it runs:** the same Cloudflare service as notifications (`push/`): one private store per person, plus one shared name book (username -> account). Email codes are sent by Resend from `daydeck.day` (key in the repository secret `RESEND_API_KEY`). Free tiers to start; Cloudflare's paid plan (about US$5 a month) once there are real users.
- **Never change the address.** Passkeys are tied to `daydeck.day`; moving to another domain would break everyone's Face ID sign-in.
- **Built so far (2026-11-20):** the service (`push/accounts.js`), and the sign-up screens and "Your account" in Settings (`js/account.js`), **on for everyone since 2026-11-20** (alex): new people see the sign-up first, people already using the app see it once, and a tester invite link goes first. People stay signed in: every use pushes the sign-in's end back to 180 days away, so Face ID is only for a new phone or after signing out. The sign-in key, username, email and picture live under `localStorage` key `daydeck-account`, apart from the days, so Save a copy never carries them. Profile pictures: everyone starts with the smile; a photo is shrunk, cleaned and kept on the phone. Backup and sync are built (`js/sync.js`, see below). `node tests/signup.test.cjs` walks the whole journey (SHOTS=1 saves a picture of each screen).
- **Later:** native iPhone and Android apps by wrapping this same app (Capacitor), signing in with the same passkeys (linked to daydeck.day); then social features on the same accounts.

## Design rules (agreed with alex; don't drift from these)
- Feeling: wonder, grown-up, calm. No mascots, confetti, badges, streaks, targets or bright saturated colours. Exception (alex 2026-11-13): cute characters are allowed as ART on an unlockable card style only; they never talk, send messages or react to what the person does or misses. Exception (alex 2026-11-20): default profile pictures are little living things (head-and-shoulders silhouettes: flower, smile, frog, cat, bunny, bear, chick, sprout), one tint on a darker shade of itself; pictures only, same rules.
- Palette: sky `#24262b / #141518 / #08090a`, ink `#efede9`, sun `#f2c08f`, gold accent `#e3c486`, pillar tints `#d9b3a6 #cfc59b #a9c4b5 #a9b9d6 #c4b4c9`.
- Font: system stack (SF Pro on Apple). Apple's standard text sizes, never scaled with the screen: large title 34, titles 22 / 20, body and buttons 17, smaller 15, notes 13, tiny labels 12 / 11 (never below 11). Only the sky scales to fill the screen.
- No decorative stars. Every star is a thought the person wrote. Kind thought = 4 points, worry = 6, want = 8. Never colour alone.
- Planets: still, set evenly around the sun (focus pillars either side). Flat, lit from the sun with a crisp shadow on the far side; surface marks, craters and bumps rotate on a sphere (all flat, same colour, light on the sun side). No orbit lines. Each name is a level ring round its planet (name twice, 13px weight 500), a lap every 20s, turning so names slide in from the left; near side readable, far side faint and mirrored behind the planet.
- Sun: the one hero, a wide warm glow that breathes slowly (about 4s in, 5s out). Greeting is quiet and centred (22). Fewer colours: only focus pillars keep their tint.
- Thoughts never pass behind the sun or a planet; sorted thoughts circle their pillar.
- Stars roam the whole open sky on slow Lissajous laps (6 to 10 minutes), starting from where they landed.
- Running a timer zooms the sky into that planet (only the planet and the passing sun remain), then the time fades in under it.
- Sorting (`js/sort.js`): one card at a time, in the sky or on a page (planets in a row above a stack of cards). Drag the card up onto a planet, or the sun ("Just me"), to sort; swipe left to let go, right for "not sure yet". No labels on screen: the words fade in behind the card while swiping. A one-line tip shows only on the first few sorts. The round × at the top right always stops sorting. Letting go sinks down and crumbles away, with a line of relief (worries: "That was a moment, not the truth."; others: "You felt it. You don't have to keep it.").
- Thoughts are always shown in quotation marks.
- One focal point per card; everything else steps down. A label is never smaller than what it labels.
- Mockups (alex 2026-11-15): alex's phone is an iPhone 15 Pro Max, 430 x 932 points (safe areas 59 top, 34 bottom). Every phone mockup is drawn in that frame at 1:1 with the app's real sizes; start from `/mnt/project-files/phone-frame/phone-frame.html`. Never draw in a smaller phone, or cards look bigger than they will on the phone.
- One 20px left edge; spacing 4 / 8 / 16 / 24 / 40. Corners: sheet 26, card 16, button 14, field 12, chip 10. Buttons that are only an icon are round.
- Tap targets at least 44px. Respect reduce-motion (stop all motion) and nothing flashes.
- Voice: comforting at hard moments (worries, reflection, repeats), brief and steady everywhere else.
- Features are revealed slowly and we say so up front.
- Not therapy: crisis support (Australian lines) must always be reachable.
- Tester mode (`js/tester.js`, dashboard `testers.html`, storage in `push/worker.js`): beta testers opt in and the app sends only choices and numbers, never typed words. **Every new feature must report what people do with `log("name", value)`** (cards drawn/passed/done/thumbs, setup steps, pillars and mindsets, levels and packs), and add its section to `testers.html` (cards, setup, pillars and levels already have "not built yet" placeholders). A new KIND of data needs a line in `SHARED` and a `CONSENT` bump so testers are asked again.
- The notifications service (`push/worker.js`) only answers the app's own addresses (`APPS`: daydeck.day, plus the old GitHub address during the move).
- Notifications (`js/notify.js`, `push/`) are a FIRST VERSION, NOT FINAL: built as first drafted at alex's request; words and design get revisited. The service only knows times, never words; a thought never goes on the lock screen.
