## What this app is (signed off by alex, 2026-11-03). Check every idea against this first.
Full one-pager: https://claude.ai/artifact/3YMBhpwDit9UNVWP2V2qHY

- **Purpose:** live with intention. Choose the few parts of life that matter, give them time, and see where your time really goes, a week at a time.
- **Built for:** people aged 18 to 30, burnt out or low (not in crisis), in Australia first: young workers early in their careers (Mia, 24) and university students (Josh, 19).
- **The rule:** a feature or fix goes in only if it helps them, or costs them nothing (no extra steps, nothing extra on screen). Others are welcome, but we don't design around them.
- **The core loop (time leads; everything else supports it):**
  1. Choose: 3 to 5 pillars, 2 of them focus, chosen on day one (the app helps people choose).
  2. Give time: tap a planet to time it, or log "I gave time" afterwards with a rough length. Both count the same.
  3. See: tap the sun any time for "Your week" (each planet's time this week). No numbers on the sky itself (alex, 2026-11-03). Lead with what happened, never gaps, comparisons or targets.
  4. Reflect: the weekly look back (the weekly recap) is always on Sunday (alex 2026-11-22: kept fixed, no choosing a day): ready once Sunday is ended, or any day after for last week, and waits on home until the person is ready.
- **Supporting features, one job each:** the sky (shows life at a glance), the weekly look back, catching thoughts (where your mind goes, beside where your time goes), sorting thoughts, words of affirmation (kind, believable lines tied to their pillars; for kind, worry and want thoughts; never for heavy thoughts), seasons, safety.
- **Not:** a productivity tracker, a journal first, or therapy.

## Tech
- The app is Daydeck, served at **https://daydeck.day** (alex, 2026-11-20): GitHub Pages with a custom domain (DNS on Cloudflare). Daydeck lives at the front of the repo; the old Intentionally app is retired and removed. `v2/` only forwards old links and icons on to the front page, keeping `?tester`.
- Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules. No build step, no frameworks, no dependencies. Open `index.html` through any static server.
- All data lives on the phone: `localStorage` under the key `daydeck` (see `js/store.js`), photos in IndexedDB (`js/photos.js`). Bump `SCHEMA` and migrate when the shape changes.
- **Photos keep nothing but the picture** (alex 2026-11-20): no location, time, phone or camera details, and no note of when a photo was taken or whether it came from the camera or the library. Every photo coming in or going out (camera, library, Restore, Save a copy, Save my photos, share, and later a profile photo) goes through `clean()` in `js/photos.js`. The app never asks for access to the photo library; it uses the phone's own picker, which hands over only the one photo chosen. `node tests/photo-clean.test.cjs` checks it.
- **Opening and coming back (alex 2026-11-21, "like other social apps"):** a fresh open shows the splash (the logo, the app icon's eclipse, on plain black, no glow; new look alex 2026-11-23; a whole sun inside the moon with a fine light rim, alex 2026-11-24, DESIGN.md 18b; styled inside `index.html` so it's there from the first moment) while home gets ready behind it (`body.booting`), then it fades and home rises in: at least 0.8s, at most about 1.5s even offline. Sheets, tips and invites wait for it (`booted` in `js/main.js`). The iPhone's own launch pictures (`icons/launch-*.png`, linked in `index.html`) are drawn from the same splash by `node tools/make-launch.cjs`; rerun it whenever the splash changes. Coming back shows Daydeck just as it was left: no splash, no reload, and home only redraws if what it shows has moved on (`lookNow()`: a new day, the greeting, or which main button). An update found while open waits for the next fresh open, or a return after 30+ minutes away (then behind the splash). `node tests/launch.test.cjs` checks it.
- **Run all the tests with `tools/test-all.sh`** (about 3 minutes: six at a time, then any failure retried on its own). While working on one screen, run only that screen's tests; the full run once before saving. Tests guard what can't be seen by looking: people's data, privacy, safety, accounts, backups and the sums behind the charts. Look and layout are checked by eye against DESIGN.md, not with new tests (alex 2026-11-24).
- `sw.js` caches the app for offline use. **Bump `VERSION` and update `FILES` whenever any file changes or is added.**
- iOS 26 lays a home-screen app out one status bar short (a strip at the bottom) unless the page is taller than that; `html` gets a `min-height` for home-screen apps in `css/app.css`. Keep it.
- Every page (`index.html`, `testers.html` and the `v2/` forwarding page) carries a Content-Security-Policy meta tag (safety net): only the app's own files and `intentionally-push.lextdo.workers.dev`. No inline `<script>` and no outside scripts; a new outside service must be added to `connect-src` in `index.html` and `testers.html`. `node tests/csp.test.cjs` checks every screen.
- Time always comes from `now()` in `js/clock.js`, never `Date.now()` directly in app logic, so test mode's time travel works. (The sky's orbit phase uses the real clock on purpose.)

## Accounts (decided with alex, 2026-11-20; live for everyone)
- **Sign up first, with a way out.** New people see "Keep your days safe" before choosing pillars: email, then a 6-digit code, then a username, then Face ID. "Use without an account" is always there; it shows one calm screen saying what they give up (days live only on this phone, so a lost phone loses them; friends and sharing will need an account) and that they can sign up any time in Settings. People already using the app see the sign-up screen once after the update; their days upload when they sign up. This is a deliberate exception to "no extra steps".
- **Signing in:** passkeys (Face ID, Touch ID or the phone passcode), with no passwords. A 6-digit email code is for first sign-up and for getting back in if the passkey is missing. Always a code, never a link: on iPhone a link would open Safari, not the home-screen app. The code box uses `autocomplete="one-time-code"` so iPhone offers the code from Mail.
- **Usernames:** 3 to 20 characters, letters, numbers, full stops and underscores only (plain a to z, so no code, emoji, invisible characters or lookalike letters from other alphabets); not starting or ending with a full stop, no two in a row, at least one letter (alex 2026-11-20, like Instagram); always lowercase (alex 2026-11-22): typed capitals become lowercase as you type, the service saves and shows them in lowercase, and names saved with capitals before then are made lowercase when the service starts, so "Mia" and "mia" can never be two people; numbers standing in for letters ("adm1n") are read as letters when checking blocked words; blocked: offensive words, anything posing as Daydeck or staff ("daydeck", "admin", "support", "official", "help"), and crisis-line names. Taken names get two or three suggestions. Can change once every 30 days; an old name stays locked for a while. Not shown to anyone until the social side exists. It doesn't have to be a real name, and the screen says so. A display name waits for the social side.
- **What the server keeps:** email, username, the passkey's public half, sessions, and each person's backed-up days; and for friends (2026-11-21), the chosen name, This season line and picture (a little one, or a small copy of the profile photo for friends, 2026-11-22), friendships, waiting requests, blocks, the 18+ tick, and invite links (scrambled). Deleting the account removes all of it. Normal accounts: protected, kept to the minimum, never read or sold; the privacy policy says so plainly. Photos and the profile photo are backed up to their own private store (see "Photos are backed up"); they still travel in Save a copy. Typed words are part of the backup, but tester mode still never sends typed words.
- **Backup and sync** (`js/sync.js`, built 2026-11-20): the phone stays the main copy and works offline; changes go up a few seconds after they're made, whenever it's online, and come back on a new phone. Everything is split into pieces (each day, and each other part like pillars or levels), and the newer piece wins, so a day changed on one phone never wipes a different day changed on another. A new phone's blank start never overwrites the backup. "Backed up 5 minutes ago" in Settings. Stays on the phone only: tester mode, test mode, notifications, photo settings, the card library's caches. Sign out keeps the phone's data. Delete everything (or a phone suddenly emptied) signs that phone out and never wipes the backup. Delete account erases everything on the server. The server refuses to overwrite a backup a phone hasn't seen; it sends it back to merge. Backup sync state lives under `localStorage` key `daydeck-sync`. `node tests/sync.test.cjs` runs two pretend phones against the real service.
- **Photos are backed up (built 2026-11-21; `js/photo-backup.js`, service `push/photos.js`).** Their own private store: Cloudflare R2 bucket **daydeck-photos** (location hint Oceania), bound as `PHOTOS` in `push/wrangler.toml`; objects `u/<account id>/<day>.jpg` and `profile.jpg`. Each photo is cleaned on the phone, shrunk to 900 by 1260, and checked again by the service (clean JPEG, under 700 KB); only its owner can read it. A day's `ph` note joins the days backup only once its photo is backed up (`ph.b`, with `ph.v` its version); a new phone fetches each picture the first time it's shown; a newer photo from another phone replaces the old copy, and one removed elsewhere is removed here. Removing a photo deletes its copy; Settings → Photos → "Back up my photos" (on by default) turns it off and deletes all copies; deleting the account deletes them all. Limits (alex 2026-11-21: not fussed about a dollar or two, but no runaway): 25 photos a day per person (older photos catching up after signing in: 200 a day), a 50 GB ceiling across everyone with an email to alex at 40 GB, and monthly save/load ceilings at R2's free amounts. Expected cost: free until roughly 180 people have a year of daily photos; about $1 a month at around 1,400. People who already had photos get a one-time note ("Your photos are backed up now", with Turn it off). `node tests/photo-backup.test.cjs` (service) and `node tests/photo-backup-app.test.cjs` (two phones against the real service).
- **Privacy policy** (`privacy.html`, daydeck.day/privacy.html, 2026-11-20): plain English, written to cover every topic the Australian Privacy Principles (APP 1.4) ask for, since Daydeck may count as a health service. Linked from the first sign-up screen and Settings → About. **Keep it true:** any change to what leaves the phone, who helps run Daydeck, or how long things are kept must update it and its date (and say so in the app if it's a big change, like backing up photos).
- **Where it runs:** the same Cloudflare service as notifications (`push/`): one private store per person, plus one shared name book (username -> account). Email codes are sent by Resend from `daydeck.day` (key in the repository secret `RESEND_API_KEY`). Free tiers to start; Cloudflare's paid plan (about US$5 a month) once there are real users.
- **Never change the address.** Passkeys are tied to `daydeck.day`; moving to another domain would break everyone's Face ID sign-in.
- **Built so far (2026-11-20):** the service (`push/accounts.js`), and the sign-up screens and "Your account" in Settings (`js/account.js`), **on for everyone since 2026-11-20** (alex): new people see the sign-up first, people already using the app see it once, and a tester invite link goes first. ## Your days: Mood and sleep (agreed with alex, 2026-11-22)
- Replaces the old dot chart. One column per night: taller = more sleep, colour = how the next day felt. **By hours** (opens first) lines the last 4 weeks up shortest to longest, with the columns sliding; **By date** shows the same nights in order. Same nights in both tabs, always the last 4 weeks (up to 28).
- Slide a finger across to read a night ("Thu 19 Nov · 5h 50m · Low ›"); tap that line to open the day. A chosen night clears itself after 4 seconds. Up and down still scroll.
- **One sleep rule** (`sleepPattern` in `js/stats.js`), used by this card and by "What seems to help you", so the hours never disagree. Whole-hour groups, best AVERAGE mood (so mostly-low weeks still show a pattern), 3 nights a group (2 in early days), about one mood step apart. Wording is always "Your best days came after …" (something that happened, never a target). Honest when there's nothing: "No clear link…", "steady", "This fills in…".
